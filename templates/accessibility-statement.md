# Accessibility statement template

A starting point for the page that the panel's `data-statement` link opens. Copy everything below the line into a page on your site (for example `/accessibility`), replace every `[bracketed]` placeholder, and delete any section that does not apply.

Only state what is true. If the site has not been checked against WCAG, say "we are working towards" rather than "conforms to". An inaccurate statement can create more legal exposure than none.

# Accessibility

*Last reviewed: [Month Year]*

[Organisation name] wants everyone to be able to use [website address], including people who are blind or have low vision, are deaf or hard of hearing, have limited movement, have cognitive or learning disabilities, or use assistive technology such as screen readers, screen magnifiers, voice control or switch devices.

## Our standard

We aim to meet the [Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/) at Level AA. These guidelines explain how to make web content easier to use for people with disabilities, and for everyone else too.

**Conformance status:** [choose one]

- *Fully conformant:* the content meets WCAG 2.1 Level AA without exceptions.
- *Partially conformant:* most of the content meets WCAG 2.1 Level AA; the exceptions are listed under "Known limitations" below.
- *Working towards conformance:* we have not yet completed a full review. We are working towards WCAG 2.1 Level AA and fix problems as we find them.

## What we do

- Write pages with headings, labels and landmarks so they make sense to screen readers.
- Make every feature work with a keyboard alone, with a visible focus outline.
- Keep text and background colours at a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1.
- Give images text alternatives, and videos captions [and transcripts].
- Let pages reflow to a narrow screen and to 200% zoom without losing content.
- [Test new pages with a screen reader (for example NVDA, VoiceOver or TalkBack) before they go live.]
- [Train the people who edit the site in writing accessible content.]

## Accessibility panel

Every page has an accessibility panel. Open it with the round button in the [lower right / lower left] corner, by pressing **Alt + Shift + A**, or with this link: <a href="#a11y">Open the accessibility panel</a>.

The panel lets you:

- **Text:** make text larger, add space between letters, words and lines, change text alignment, and switch to an easier-to-read font.
- **Colour:** switch to high contrast, a dark theme or inverted colours, and make colours stronger or turn them to greyscale.
- **Reading aids:** highlight links and headings, show a stronger focus outline, use a larger cursor, add a reading guide or reading mask, stop animations, hide images, and mute media.
- **Tools:** have the page read aloud, and jump to any heading or section from a page outline.
- **Profiles:** turn on a set of settings with one tap: Vision impaired, Dyslexia & reading, ADHD & focus, Seizure safe, and Keyboard & motor.

Your choices are saved only in your own browser, so they stay in place on every page and on your next visit. They are never sent to us. The panel works in English, Chinese, Spanish and Vietnamese.

The panel adds to, and does not replace, the accessibility built into the site. Your own device settings, browser zoom and assistive technology will always work as well.

## Compatibility

The site is designed to work with:

- Recent versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on desktop and mobile.
- Screen readers including JAWS and NVDA on Windows, VoiceOver on macOS and iOS, and TalkBack on Android.
- Browser zoom up to 400%, and operating system text-size settings.
